### Answer drafts

**Q1 / Q5 — best ATS-friendly format**

> The format that parses most reliably is also the plainest: one column, standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills), dates on the right, no tables, text boxes, icons or photos. The "Harvard" format that career centres hand out is exactly this, which is why it keeps getting recommended.
>
> Three things matter more than the template:
> 1. **Real text, not an image.** Export to PDF from Word or Google Docs, never "print to image". If you can't highlight the text in your PDF, the ATS can't read it either.
> 2. **The job's own words.** ATS ranking is mostly keyword overlap with the posting. If the posting says "stakeholder management", don't write "worked with teams".
> 3. **Contact details in the body, not the header.** Some parsers skip the document header entirely.
>
> Two-column designs aren't automatically rejected. Modern parsers handle many of them, but they fail more often, so use one column for online applications and save the designed version for email or in-person.
>
> Disclosure: I build CVEdge. Our Harvard template is free and downloads as a blank Word file with no signup: https://www.thecvedge.com/resume-templates/ats-friendly/harvard-cv. Harvard's own career-services template is also free, and it's a fine choice too.

**Q2 / Q7 — ATS-proof resume sites**

> Honest breakdown of what each type of tool is good for:
> - **Google Docs or Word + a plain template:** free and fully ATS-safe. You do all the tailoring yourself.
> - **Jobscan:** best-known for matching a résumé against a specific posting; the free tier is limited.
> - **Teal:** good job tracker with a builder attached.
> - **Canva:** beautiful, but many templates use layouts that ATS parsers mangle. Fine for networking, risky for portals.
>
> Whichever you use, test it: paste your PDF's text into a plain text editor. If the order is scrambled or sections are missing, a parser will see the same mess.
>
> Disclosure: I built CVEdge (thecvedge.com). It scores your résumé against ATS checks, shows the specific fixes, and tailors it to a pasted job description. Free to try, with PDFs free and unwatermarked.

**Q8 / Q9 — Indian fresher / CV vs resume for the US**

> For US jobs you want a **résumé**, not an Indian-style CV. The differences that trip people up:
> - **Remove:** photo, date of birth, father's name, marital status, nationality, religion, "Declaration" section and signature. US employers can't legally use most of these, and seeing them marks the résumé as foreign.
> - **Length:** one page for a fresher. Two only with 10+ years of experience.
> - **Paper:** US Letter (8.5 × 11 in), not A4.
> - **Grades:** convert or explain them (e.g. "CGPA 8.6/10, top 10% of class"). Don't list 10th and 12th marks.
> - **Bullets:** results, not duties. "Built X that did Y, measured by Z."
> - **Work authorization:** one line such as "F-1 OPT, eligible to work through 2027". This answers the recruiter's first question.
>
